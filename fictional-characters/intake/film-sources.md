# Film candidate source and selection notes

## Corpus and rights

- CMU Movie Summary Corpus, official landing page: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ark/personas/
- Dataset README: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ark/personas/data/README.txt
- Research citation: David Bamman, Brendan O’Connor, Noah A. Smith, “Learning Latent Personas of Film Characters,” ACL 2013: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dbamman/pubs/pdf/bamman%2Boconnor%2Bsmith.acl13.pdf
- The CMU page and README state Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike; the linked license is CC BY-SA 3.0 US: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/us/ . Attribute the corpus creators and dataset, retain source URLs and this license notice. The corpus contains plot summaries extracted from the 2012 English Wikipedia dump; each candidate includes its original Wikipedia page ID URL and hash of the source row. Corpus data is not a license to reproduce film scripts or other unprovided works.

The official README says the corpus contains 42,306 plot summaries from the November 2, 2012 English-Wikipedia dump; movie metadata (81,741 rows) and character metadata (450,669 rows) were extracted from the November 4, 2012 Freebase dump. It lists the character columns separately: character/actor map ID is column 11 and Freebase character ID is column 12. The build script uses column 12 only; actor identity, age, gender, height and ethnicity are never written to candidate records.

## Selection boundary

`film_build_candidates.py` keeps records with a parsed release year, positive numeric box-office value, at least 150 English-token words in the plot, at least two source character entities with stable IDs, and at least two names/aliases linked to the plot by literal mention. It excludes records whose Freebase genre metadata identifies a documentary, concert film, music video, or short film/subject. The output keeps the complete Freebase character-entity inventory (column 12), sorted by a weak mention hint; first/last-name variants are counted to reduce alias misses such as “Jake” for “Jake Sully.” Mention counts are only review hints, never proof of lead status. No actor fields or physical/demographic traits are output.

The first 500 retained entries are the top raw numeric box-office values among eligible records in this filtered historical snapshot. The next 100 are the highest raw-revenue eligible backup records after that tranche with no United States production-country label, widening the market/language pool while also supplying replacements if a candidate later fails review. Each record keeps its raw revenue rank among all eligible snapshot records. The CMU README does not specify a consistent currency/unit for that field, and values are not inflation-adjusted. The result is not a current global ranking. Reported language and production-country labels are included verbatim from metadata; the corpus remains English-Wikipedia-centered and uneven in coverage. The main 500 alone still strongly reflect United States market/coverage selection, so the backed-up 100 does not make the complete dataset globally representative.

The `source_text` field reproduces the relevant corpus plot summary for traceability and subsequent character analysis. `film-exclusions.jsonl` records explicit non-target genre and severe source-text quality exclusions. Other text that merely ends abruptly is flagged for review rather than silently rewritten. Plot summaries are concise secondary descriptions, not screenplay/dialogue or first-person character evidence. They support candidate selection and later verification, but alone should not justify stable personality claims. Where genre labels identify a biographical film, `depicted_person_caution` signals that the role may portray a real person; it is not a license to infer that person's real psychology.

## Final sampling plan

The final pool starts with 300 eligible high-revenue snapshot records and 200 distinct supplemental records: 40 with China/Hong Kong/Taiwan production labels, 100 other non-U.S. production records, and 60 further global records with a Chinese-language appearance label. A language appearance label does not establish a Chinese original-language film. Review failures are replaced with other eligible records until 500 reviewed films are retained. Tail groups only change processing batch size. Final production-region labels are retained per work and summarized in the build receipt; co-productions can count in more than one region. This is deliberately varied historical coverage, not a representative global population or a current top-500 ranking.
