## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：Walt Kowalski , a gruff retired Polish American factory worker and war veteran, has recently been widowed after 50 years of marriage.
- **S2**：His Highland Park neighborhood in Detroit, formerly populated by working-class white families, is now dominated by poor Asian immigrants, and gang violence is commonplace.
- **S3**：Adding to the isolation he feels is the emotional detachment of his family.
- **S4**：He rejects a suggestion from one of his sons to move to a retirement community , and lives alone with his dog.
- **S5**：Walt suffers from coughing fits, occasionally coughing up blood, but conceals this from his family.
- **S6**：A young Catholic priest tries to comfort him, but Walt disdains the young, inexperienced man.
- **S7**：The Hmong Vang Lor family reside next door to Walt.
- **S8**：Initially, he wants nothing to do with his new neighbors, particularly after he catches Thao Vang Lor, a member of that family, attempting to steal his Ford Gran Torino as a coerced initiation into a Hmong gang run by Thao's cousin, "Spider." The gang is infuriated and attacks Thao, but Walt confronts them with a rifle and chases them off, earning the respect of the Hmong community.
- **S9**：As penance, Thao's mother makes him work for Walt, who has him do odd jobs around the neighborhood, and the two form a grudging mutual respect.
- **S10**：Walt helps Thao get a job and gives him advice on dating.
- **S11**：The gang continues to pressure Thao and again assaults him on his way home from work.
- **S12**：Walt sees Thao's injuries and visits the gang members' house where he attacks a gang member.
- **S13**：In retaliation, the gang performs a drive-by shooting on the Vang Lor home, injuring Thao, and kidnapping and raping his sister.
- **S14**：The next day, Thao seeks Walt's help to exact revenge, who tells him to return later in the afternoon.
- **S15**：In the meantime, Walt makes personal preparations: he buys a suit, gets a haircut and makes a confession to Father Janovich.
- **S16**：When Thao returns, Walt takes him to the basement and gives him his Silver Star; Walt then locks Thao in his basement and tells him that he has been haunted by the memory of killing an enemy soldier  and insists that Thao must never be haunted with the experience of killing another man.
- **S17**：Walt then goes to the house of the gang members where they draw their weapons on him.
- **S18**：He talks loudly, berating them and drawing the attention of the neighbors.
- **S19**：Putting a cigarette in his mouth, he asks for a light; he then puts his hand in his jacket and provocatively pulls it out as if it is a gun, inciting the gang members to shoot and kill him.
- **S20**：As he falls to the ground, his hand opens to reveal an army lighter: he was unarmed.
- **S21**：Sue frees Thao and they drive to the scene in Walt's Gran Torino.
- **S22**：A Hmong police officer tells them the gang will be arrested for murder and imprisoned for a long time.
- **S23**：Walt's funeral Mass is celebrated by the young priest, Father Janovich, and attended by his family and many of the Hmong community.
- **S24**：Afterward, his last will and testament is read, and to the surprise of his family, Walt leaves them nothing: his house goes to the church and his cherished Gran Torino to Thao.
- **S25**：As the film ends, Thao is seen driving the car along Lakeshore Drive with Walt's dog, Daisy.
