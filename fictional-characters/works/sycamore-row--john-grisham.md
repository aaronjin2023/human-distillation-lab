## 材料限制

- 摘要对Seth的遗嘱安排与历史动机交代充分，对Jake的内心与庭审行动较少，其倾向仅限受托和解情境。
- 针对Lettie的诱导继承与性关系说法属于争讼中的指控，不能作为她的人格事实。

- **S4**：An employee of wealthy recluse Seth Hubbard is instructed to meet his boss at a location by these sycamores one early Sunday afternoon.

- **S6**：Hubbard has hanged himself from the tree because his terminal lung cancer had become too painful.
- **S7**：Accompanying the body are very specific funeral and burial instructions.
- **S8**：Jake Brigance, Carl Lee's former attorney, receives a letter sent by Hubbard just before he killed himself, containing a new holographic will that renounces a will he filed the year before in which he leaves all his assets to his daughter and son as well as his grandchildren.
- **S9**：In this new will, Hubbard stipulates that his children will receive nothing.
- **S10**：Instead, five percent will be given to the local church and another five percent will be left to his long-disappeared brother, Ancil Hubbard.
- **S11**：The remaining ninety percent is to be given to his black housekeeper Letitia "Lettie" Lang.
- **S12**：Further instructions stipulate that the will must not be filed for probate until after Hubbard's funeral so that his children, who rarely visited him during his bout with cancer, can put on a show not knowing that they will ultimately be left with nothing.
- **S13**：Hubbard notes that his children will certainly contest the new will because they are greedy and that Jake must do whatever it takes to make sure the new will is enforced.
- **S14**：He says he chose Jake because of the admirable work that Jake did during the Hailey trial.
- **S15**：Soon, Jake finds out that Hubbard had earned more than $20 million in a lumber yard business, a fortune unmatched by any other individual in Ford County.
- **S16**：As the executor publishes this sum, the entire town of Clanton shifts their attention to the case.
- **S17**：Hubbard's children attempt to contest their father's new will by claiming he was not capable when filing it, igniting a hotly contested court battle with many twists and turns.
- **S18**：At first the trial goes well until the opposing lawyer manages to spring a surprise witness, whose testimony seems to show that Lettie had tried to influence an earlier ailing employer to leave her money in a will, creating a suspicion of her systematically preying on the weakness of elderly ailing people.
- **S19**：Still another surprise witness, a former black female employee with whom Hubbard had sexual relations comes forward, implying that Lettie had also slept with Hubbard.
- **S20**：At the last moment, the trial is changed again by a sensational deposition given by Hubbard's long-lost brother Ancil.
- **S21**：Ancil had left Ford County and joined the U.S.
- **S22**：Navy at the age of 17, vowing never to return.
- **S23**：Since then, he had led an adventurous and often criminal life until finally being located as a bartender in Juneau, Alaska , where Lucien, Jake's friend and ex-partner, managed to obtain his testimony.
- **S24**：Ancil explains why Seth left the money to Lettie.
- **S25**：In the 1920s, Lettie's grandfather Sylvester, whom she never knew, owned a considerable plot of land.
- **S26**：His being a landowner was very much a rarity for a black person in the segregationist Deep South , and was greatly resented by racist whites in general and in particular by his neighbor, Cleon Hubbard, who laid a claim to Sylvester's land.
- **S27**：Hubbard, an abusive man who was often violent toward his wife and two sons, Seth and Ancil, tried to go to court but was unsuccessful.
- **S28**：Having failed in court, Hubbard resorted to the alternative method available at the time to whites in the Deep South, i.e. lynch law .
- **S29**：Sylvester was falsely accused of "speaking rudely to white women", which, together with resentment at his being a black landowner, was enough to mobilize a lynch mob.
- **S30**：Several men dragged Sylvester from his home and hanged him from a sycamore tree.
- **S31**：His sons, Ancil and Seth, who did not share their father's prejudice and sometimes played with the black children, secretly observed this scene with great horror.
- **S32**：Subsequently, Cleon Hubbard intimidated Esther, Lettie's grandmother, and forced her to sign away the family's ownership for a pittance – with a promise that she could continue residing on the property.
- **S33**：However, the promise was promptly broken, and Cleon and the sheriff then expelled the entire extended family and set fire to their homes and small chapel, totally eradicating the small black community which had been known as "Sycamore Row".
- **S34**：Esther, along with her five-year-old daughter (who would become Lettie's mother), had to escape with virtually no possessions.
- **S35**：An older child, with whom the Hubbard boys sometimes played, drowned in a river during the final expulsion.
- **S36**：Years later, Seth Hubbard used the property gained by his father as collateral for a mortgage in order to build his lumber yard.
- **S37**：Knowing that his success was partly owed to this mortgage and wanting to make up for the injustice caused by his father, he decided to give the majority of his capital to Lettie and in a final act hanged himself from the same tree from which Lettie's grandfather was hanged.
