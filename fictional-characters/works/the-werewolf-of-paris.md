- **S1**：## Plot Summary
- **S2**：[ edit ] Like much Gothic fiction, The Werewolf of Paris opens with a frame story in which the author explains his struggle with the fantastic elements of his tale.
- **S3**：Here the narrator, an anonymous American working on his doctoral research in Paris, discovers a manuscript in the hands of some trash-pickers.
- **S4**：He describes it as "the Galliez report: thirty four sheets of closely written French, an unsolicited defense of Sergeant Bertrand Caillet at the latter's court-martial in 1871." [ 3 ] A descendant of the cursed Pitamont clan, which destroyed itself in a long feud with the neighboring Pitavals, Bertrand is born one Christmas Eve to an adolescent girl who had been raped by a priest, Father Pitamont.
- **S5**：Bertrand grows up with strange sadistic and sexual desires which are usually expressed as dreams.
- **S6**：Sometimes the dreams are memories of actual experiences in which he had transformed into a wolf.
- **S7**：His step-uncle, Aymar Galliez, who raises the boy (along with his mother Josephine and a servant Françoise), soon learns of Bertrand's affliction.
- **S8**：Bertrand flees to Paris after his assault on a prostitute, his incestuous union with his mother, and his murder of a friend in their home village.
- **S9**：Aymar tries to find Bertrand by studying the details of local crimes, such as the mutilation of corpses and various murders.
- **S10**：Bertrand joins the National Guard during the Franco-Prussian War , doing little fighting and finding love from a girl who volunteers at a canteen, the beautiful and wealthy Sophie de Blumenberg.
- **S11**：Sophie, a masochist and obsessed with death, helps Bertrand avoid the violent effects of his transformation by allowing him to cut into her flesh in order to suck her blood.
- **S12**：Aymar finds Bertrand in Paris during the Paris Commune , but thinking that love has cured Bertrand, he decides not to take action.
- **S13**：Fearing that he'll accidentally kill Sophie, Bertrand goes out one night to feed on someone else.
- **S14**：He is caught attacking a fellow soldier and arrested.
- **S15**：Aymar supports burning Bertrand at the stake, and provides the court with a summary of Bertrand's crimes, but the court sentences him to treatment at the infirmary of La Santé prison .
- **S16**：Aymar transfers Bertrand to an asylum after the reactionary Versaillists have retaken Paris, with great loss of life among the Communards , who are executed en masse.
- **S17**：Unbeknownst to Aymar, Bertrand suffers in a small cell, drugged when he is visited by his uncle.
- **S18**：Bertrand eventually commits suicide by jumping from the building with another inmate whom he delusively believes is Sophie.
- **S19**：Their deaths are similar to a suicide fantasy that Bertrand and Sophie enjoyed; the real Sophie had previously committed suicide on her own, unable to deal with her separation from Bertrand.
- **S20**：The narrative proper is followed by a grisly appendix citing a municipal report on the cemeteries of Paris.
- **S21**：The report indicates that the grave of one "Sieur C ... (Bertrand)" contained the body "of a dog, which despite 8 years in the ground was still incompletely destroyed." [ 4 ]
