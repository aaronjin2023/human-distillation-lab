## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] In 1970, a 23-year-old college dropout named Willie Traynor realizes that his dreams of becoming a Pulitzer -winning journalist will never come true.
- **S3**：He moves to Clanton, Mississippi for an internship at the local newspaper, The Ford County Times .
- **S4**：However, the aging editor, Wilson Caudle, drives the newspaper into bankruptcy through years of neglect and mismanagement.
- **S5**：Willie spontaneously decides to buy the paper for $50,000, using money borrowed from his wealthy grandmother, and becomes the editor and owner of the Times .
- **S6**：Soon afterwards, Danny Padgitt, a member of a notorious local family, brutally rapes and murders a young widow, Rhoda Kassellaw.
- **S7**：When Traynor publishes a front-page photo of the blood-spattered Padgitt being led into jail, readership increases.
- **S8**：However, Willie is accused of yellow journalism and pre-judging Padgitt.
- **S9**：Later, Willie runs a human interest story about Callie Ruffin, a local black woman whose seven children (among eight) all gained doctorates and teaching positions in various universities.
- **S10**：In the process of researching the story, Willie becomes a close friend of Callie and her family.
- **S11**：In the process of jury selection for Padgitt's trial, Callie becomes the first black person to serve on a Ford County jury.
- **S12**：Though far from enthusiastic about the prospect of having to pass a death sentence , Callie - who had been active of the Civil Rights Movement - does not shirk her civic duty.
- **S13**：In court, Padgitt openly threatens to kill each of the jurors if he's convicted.
- **S14**：But though the jury convicts Padgitt, they are divided on whether to sentence him to death row , so he is sentenced to life imprisonment at the Mississippi State Penitentiary .
- **S15**：Unconfirmed rumors persist that Hank Hooten, the deputy prosecutor in the case, had been the lover of the victim - which, if true, would constitute a conflict of interest .
- **S16**：In the ensuing years, as the Times becomes highly successful and steadily increases circulation, Willie keeps an eye on Padgitt.
- **S17**：He campaigns against the extremely favorable conditions which his family procured for him in prison.
- **S18**：However, Padgitt is paroled after nine years.
- **S19**：Immediately after his return to Clanton, two of the former jurors are killed by a sniper rifle and fear spreads through the county.
- **S20**：Callie's children and neighbors organize to guard her day and night.
- **S21**：The Padgitt family offers alibis as to Danny's whereabouts during the murders.
- **S22**：This is taken with great skepticism, but with no tangible proof to the contrary, the authorities hesitate to take action.
- **S23**：Callie reveals that the victims were jurors who voted against sentencing Padgitt to death.
- **S24**：A third juror - who also opposed a death sentence - narrowly escapes a bomb sent through the mail.
- **S25**：The resulting public uproar forces the authorities' hands, and a warrant is issued for Padgitt's arrest.
- **S26**：Against expectations, Padgitt surrenders to police.
- **S27**：During his bail hearing, however, Padgitt is shot and killed by Hooten, who is hiding in the ceiling.
- **S28**：Hooten barricades himself in the courthouse tower and, after a short standoff, commits suicide.
- **S29**：It is revealed that Padgitt - though guilty of his original crime - was innocent of the murders.
- **S30**：Willie discovers that Hooten was indeed involved with Rhoda Kassellaw, and sought revenge against Padgitt and the three jurors who voted against his death sentence.
- **S31**：A big newspaper chain, extending operations throughout the South, becomes interested in purchasing the Times .
- **S32**：Willie sells the paper for $1.5 million, but his satisfaction is marred when Callie dies of a heart attack .
- **S33**：The book ends with Willie writing her obituary.
