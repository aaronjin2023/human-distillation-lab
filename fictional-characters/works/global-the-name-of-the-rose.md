- **S1**：## Plot Summary
- **S2**：[ edit ] In 1327, Franciscan friar William of Baskerville and his assistant Adso of Melk arrive at a Benedictine abbey in Northern Italy to attend a theological disputation .
- **S3**：The abbey is being used as neutral ground in a dispute between Pope John XXII and the Franciscans over the question of apostolic poverty .
- **S4**：The monks of the abbey have recently been shaken by the suspicious death of one of their brothers, Adelmo of Otranto, and the abbot asks William (a former inquisitor ) to investigate the incident.
- **S5**：During his inquiries, William has a debate with one of the oldest monks in the abbey, Jorge of Burgos, about the permissibility of laughter, which Jorge regards as a threat to God's established order.
- **S6**：The second day, another monk, Venantius of Salvemec, is found dead in a vat of pig's blood.
- **S7**：He has black stains on his tongue and fingers, suggesting poison.
- **S8**：William learns that Adelmo was part of a homosexual love triangle that also involved the librarian, Malachi of Hildesheim, and Malachi's assistant, Berengar of Arundel.
- **S9**：The only other monks who knew about these indiscretions were Jorge and Venantius.
- **S10**：In spite of Malachi's ban, William and Adso enter the abbey's labyrinthine library.
- **S11**：They discover that the library contains a hidden room named the finis Africae after the presumed edge of the world, but they are unable to locate it.
- **S12**：In the scriptorium , they find a book on Venantius's desk along with some cryptic notes.
- **S13**：Someone snatches the book and they pursue to no avail.
- **S14**：The third day, the monks are surprised by the disappearance of Berengar and William learns that there are two former Dulcinians in the abbey (Remigio of Varagine, the abbey's cellarer , and the deformed monk Salvatore).
- **S15**：Adso returns to the library alone in the evening.
- **S16**：While leaving the library through the kitchen, he encounters a peasant girl.
- **S17**：Although they do not share a language, they have a sexual encounter, Adso's first.
- **S18**：After confessing to William, Adso is absolved, although he still feels guilty.
- **S19**：The fourth day, Berengar is found drowned in the abbey's bathhouse.
- **S20**：His fingers and tongue bear stains similar to those found on Venantius.
- **S21**：The pope's legation now arrives, led by Grand Inquisitor Bernard Gui .
- **S22**：Salvatore is discovered attempting to cast a primitive love spell on the peasant girl, and Bernard arrests them both for witchcraft and heresy.
- **S23**：The fifth day is the day of the disputation.
- **S24**：Severinus, the abbey's herbalist, tells William that he has found a "strange book" that demands the friar's attention, but William is unable to investigate the discovery until the disputation has ended.
- **S25**：When William and Adso arrive at Severinus's laboratory, they find him dead, his skull crushed by a heavy armillary sphere .
- **S26**：They search the room for the missing book but are unable to locate it.
- **S27**：Remigio is discovered at the scene of the crime and taken into custody by Bernard, who accuses the "heretic" of committing all four homicides.
- **S28**：Under threat of torture, Remigio confesses.
- **S29**：Remigio, Salvatore, and the peasant girl are taken away and assumed to be doomed.
- **S30**：In response to the recent tragedies in the abbey, Jorge gives an apocalyptic sermon about the coming of the Antichrist .
- **S31**：At matins the morning of the sixth day, Malachi drops dead, his fingers and tongue black.
- **S32**：The abbot is distraught at William's failure to solve the crimes and orders him to leave the abbey the following day.
- **S33**：That night, William and Adso penetrate the library once more and enter the finis Africae by solving Venantius's riddle.
- **S34**：They discover Jorge waiting for them in the forbidden room.
- **S35**：William has by now arrived at a solution.
- **S36**：Berengar revealed the existence of the finis Africae to Adelmo in exchange for a sexual favour.
- **S37**：Adelmo, stricken with guilt over this sinful bargain, then committed suicide.
- **S38**：Venantius overheard the secret and used it to gain possession of a rare and valuable book that Jorge had hidden in the room.
- **S39**：Unbeknownst to him, Jorge had laced its pages with poison, correctly assuming that a reader would have to lick his fingers in order to turn them.
- **S40**：Venantius's body was discovered by Berengar, who, fearing exposure, disposed of it in pig's blood before claiming the book and succumbing to its poison.
- **S41**：The book was next found by Severinus, but Jorge manipulated Malachi into killing him before he could pass it on to William.
- **S42**：Malachi died after ignoring Jorge's warning not to investigate the book's contents.
- **S43**：The book itself, now back in Jorge's possession, is the lost second half of Aristotle's Poetics , which discusses the virtues of laughter.
- **S44**：Jorge confirms William's deductions and justifies himself by pointing to the fact that the deaths correspond to the seven trumpets described in the Book of Revelation , and therefore must form part of a divine plan.
- **S45**：Two more deaths will complete the sequence: that of the abbot, whom Jorge has trapped in an airless passageway beneath the finis Africae , and that of Jorge himself.
- **S46**：He begins consuming the book's poisoned pages and uses Adso's lantern to start a fire in the library.
- **S47**：Adso summons the monks in a futile attempt to extinguish the fire.
- **S48**：As the fire consumes the library and spreads to the rest of the abbey, William laments his failure.
- **S49**：Confused and defeated, William and Adso escape the abbey.
- **S50**：Years later, Adso, now aged, returns to the ruins of the abbey and salvages any remaining scraps and fragments, eventually creating a lesser library.
