## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] Wendall Rohr and his team of tort lawyers have filed suit on behalf of plaintiff Celeste Wood, whose husband died of lung cancer , against the tobacco company Pynex.
- **S3**：The trial is to be held in Biloxi , Mississippi , a state thought to have favorable tort laws and sympathetic juries .
- **S4**：Before the jury in the Pynex trial has been sworn in, a stealth juror , Nicholas Easter, has successfully gotten himself added to the jury, in concert with a mysterious woman known only as Marlee.
- **S5**：Rankin Fitch, a shady " consultant " who has directed eight successful trials for the tobacco industry, has placed a camera in the courtroom in order to observe the proceedings in his office nearby, where he and his team analyze the jury and plan to manipulate them.
- **S6**：He plans to get to Millie Dupree through blackmailing her husband through a tape that has him trying to bribe an official.
- **S7**：He gets to Lonnie Shaver by convincing a company to buy his employer and convince him through orientation.
- **S8**：He also tries to reach Rikki Coleman through blackmail of revealing her abortion to her husband.
- **S9**：As the case continues, Fitch is approached by Marlee with a proposal to "buy" the verdict.
- **S10**：Meanwhile, Easter works from the inside to gain control of the jury – being warm-hearted, sympathetic and helpful to jurors who might be won over, and ruthless to those who prove impervious to his efforts.
- **S11**：Eventually, he becomes jury foreman after the previous one falls ill (resulting from Easter spiking his coffee).
- **S12**：He also manages to hoodwink and repeatedly manipulate the judge.
- **S13**：Meanwhile, Marlee – Easter's partner/lover – acts as his agent on the outside, convincing Fitch, through a series of highly secretive meetings, that Easter controls the jury and can deliver any verdict on demand.
- **S14**：Marlee gives Fitch the impression that the pair's objective is purely mercenary – to sell the verdict to highest bidder.
- **S15**：Still, he makes a great effort to discover her true name and antecedents to satisfy his mounting curiosity.
- **S16**：This turns out to be extremely difficult, and the detectives employed by Fitch express their grudging respect for her skill in concealing her tracks.
- **S17**：As the trial reaches its climax, Fitch – still in the dark about Marlee's past – agrees to her proposal to pay $10 million for a favorable verdict.
- **S18**：Only after the money is irrevocably transferred to an offshore account do the detectives discover the truth: Marlee is in fact an anti-smoking activist whose parents both died from tobacco use.
- **S19**：Thus, Fitch realizes that he lost Pynex's $10 million in addition to having lost the trial.
- **S20**：Inside the closed jury room, Easter convinces the jury to find for the plaintiff and make a large monetary award – $2 million in compensatory damages and $400 million in punitive damages .
- **S21**：While not able to sway the entire jury, Easter gets nine out of twelve jurors to back him – sufficient for a valid verdict in a civil case .
- **S22**：Pynex and its defense lawyers are devastated.
- **S23**：In the Cayman Islands , Marlee short-sells the tobacco companies' stocks and makes an enormous gain on the original $10 million.
- **S24**：Easter quickly disappears from Biloxi and leaves the U.S.
- **S25**：While Easter and Marlee are now wealthy and satisfied that they served justice, the tobacco industry, once undefeatable, is now vulnerable to an avalanche of additional lawsuits.
- **S26**：The book closes with Marlee returning the initial $10 million bribe to Fitch, having almost doubled the money on the derivatives market , and warning Fitch that she and Easter will always be watching.
- **S27**：She explains that she had no intention to steal or lie, and that she cheated only because, "That was all your client understood."
