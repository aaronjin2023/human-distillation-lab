## 角色资料

### Tom Joad

角色职责：protagonist · 身份：`identity-49f3ddfe94e9519c` · 独立版本：`profile-92845d847b905e37`

- **性格倾向**：家人迁徙求生时，他倾向于承担共同风险，并逐渐接过领导责任。（行为推断；S12、S47）
- **核心动机**：准备离开家人时，他明确立志为受压迫者行动。（摘要直接叙述；S31）
- **决策方式**：面对追捕风险，他倾向于先隐蔽避险，藏身暴露后再离开家人。（行为推断；S30、S31、S74）
- **关系模式**：他接纳童年认识的凯西同行，并在目睹其被杀后杀死一名袭击者。（摘要直接叙述；S3、S12、S28）

暂无支持：价值取舍、情绪反应、沟通方式、成长变化

矛盾与变化：逐渐承担家庭领导责任，却因追捕危险而必须离开家人。

### Ma Joad

角色职责：co-lead · 身份：`identity-577e4dd8bec658c1` · 独立版本：`profile-0d31cd02bad89c28`

- **性格倾向**：家人接连遭遇丧失时，她倾向于务实而坚韧地维持家庭运转。（行为推断；S17、S34、S49）
- **核心动机**：她努力把家人维系在一起，在故乡已无依靠时带领剩余成员前行。（摘要直接叙述；S17、S49）
- **价值取舍**：面对濒死陌生人时，她的关切超出自家生存，支持女儿提供救助。（行为推断；S37、S38、S39、S40）
- **成长变化**：丈夫因失去生计而崩溃后，她承担起家庭领导责任。（摘要直接叙述；S53）
- **沟通方式**：救助饥饿男子时，她与女儿仅通过目光便形成默契。（摘要直接叙述；S38、S39、S40）

暂无支持：决策方式、情绪反应、关系模式

### Jim Casy

角色职责：major-support · 身份：`identity-5f4735121c17061d` · 独立版本：`profile-4d776e4f9b7d408c`

- **性格倾向**：在工人遭受压迫时，他倾向于主动介入，而非停留在旁观位置。（行为推断；S21、S24）
- **核心动机**：面对劳动剥削，他成为组织者并尝试招募工人加入工会。（摘要直接叙述；S24）
- **价值取舍**：警员准备射杀逃跑工人时，他将保护工人置于服从警权之上。（行为推断；S21）
- **决策方式**：面对劳工困境，他既会直接阻止暴力，也采取组织联合的办法。（行为推断；S21、S24）

暂无支持：情绪反应、关系模式、沟通方式、成长变化

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] The narrative begins just after Tom Joad is paroled from McAlester prison , where he had been incarcerated after being convicted of homicide , although he insisted that he acted in self-defense.
- **S3**：While hitchhiking to his home near Sallisaw, Oklahoma , Tom meets former preacher Jim Casy, whom he remembers from childhood, and the two travel together.
- **S4**：Arriving at Tom's childhood farm home, they find it deserted.
- **S5**：Disconcerted and confused, Tom and Casy meet an old neighbor, Muley Graves, who says the family is at Uncle John Joad's home nearby.
- **S6**：Graves says the banks have evicted all the farmers.
- **S7**：They have moved away, but Muley refuses to leave the area.
- **S8**：The next morning, Tom and Casy go to Uncle John's.
- **S9**：Tom's family is loading their remaining possessions into a Hudson sedan converted into a truck; with the crops destroyed by the Dust Bowl , the family has defaulted on their bank loans and their farm has been repossessed.
- **S10**：The family sees no option but to seek work in California, which has been described in handbills as fruitful and offering high pay.
- **S11**：The Joads put everything they have into making the journey.
- **S12**：Although leaving Oklahoma violates his parole, Tom takes the risk, and invites Casy to join the family.
- **S13**：Traveling west on Route 66 , the Joads find the road crowded with other migrants.
- **S14**：In makeshift camps, they hear many stories from others, some returning from California.
- **S15**：The group worries that California may not be as rewarding as suggested.
- **S16**：The family dwindles on the way: Grampa dies of a stroke and they bury him in a field; Granma dies of illness close to the California state line; and both Noah (the eldest Joad son) and Connie Rivers (the husband of the pregnant Joad daughter, Rose of Sharon) leave the family.
- **S17**：Led by Ma, the remaining members continue on, as nothing is left for them in Oklahoma.
- **S18**：Reaching California, they find the state oversupplied with labor ; wages are low, and workers are exploited to the point of starvation.
- **S19**：The big corporate farmers are in collusion and smaller farmers suffer from collapsing prices.
- **S20**：All police and state law enforcement authorities are allied with the growers.
- **S21**：At the first migrant Hooverville camp the Joads stop at, Casy is arrested for knocking down a deputy sheriff who is about to shoot a fleeing worker for alerting others that the labor recruiter, travelling with the officer, will not pay the wages he is promising.
- **S22**：Weedpatch Camp , one of the clean, utility-supplied camps operated by the Resettlement Administration , a New Deal agency, offers better conditions but does not have enough resources to care for all the needy families, and it does not provide work or food.

- **S25**：The Joads find work as strikebreakers in a peach orchard.
- **S26**：After picking for most of the day, they are only paid enough to buy food for that night's supper and some for the next day.
- **S27**：One night, Tom meets Casy, who is confronted by two deputies who want to punish him for inciting a strike.

- **S31**：After Ruthie taunts that her brother has killed two men, Tom, knowing he must leave to avoid capture, bids his mother farewell and vows to work for the oppressed.
- **S32**：The family continues to pick cotton and pool their daily wages to buy food.
- **S33**：Upon its birth, Rose of Sharon's baby is stillborn .
- **S34**：Ma Joad remains steadfast and forces the family through the bereavement.
- **S35**：With the winter rains, the Joads' dwelling is flooded and the car disabled, and they move to higher ground.
- **S36**：The family takes shelter from the flood in an old barn.
- **S37**：Inside they find a young boy and his father, who is dying of starvation .
- **S38**：Ma realizes there is only one way to save the man.
- **S39**：She looks at Rose of Sharon and a silent understanding passes between them.
- **S40**：Rose of Sharon, left alone with the man, goes to him and has him drink her breast milk.
- **S41**：Interchapters [ edit ] Throughout the novel, short vignettes or "interchapters" describe the wider setting of the story.
- **S42**：In one of these, a land turtle attempts to cross a dusty road.
- **S43**：The turtle is not directly related to the overall story of the Joad family, but is generally symbolic of the slow persistence of the Joads and the Okies and others during the Dust Bowl and Great Depression years.

- **S45**：## Characters
- **S46**：[ edit ] Tom Joad : the protagonist of the story; the Joad family's second son, named after his father.
- **S47**：Later, Tom takes leadership of the family, even though he is young.
- **S48**：Ma Joad : the Joad family matriarch.
- **S49**：Practical but warm-spirited, she tries to hold the family together.
- **S50**：Her given name is never learned; it is suggested that her maiden name was Hazlett.
- **S51**：Pa Joad : the Joad family patriarch, also named Tom, age 50.
- **S52**：Hardworking sharecropper and family man.
- **S53**：Pa becomes a broken man upon losing his livelihood and means of supporting his family, forcing Ma to assume leadership.
- **S54**：Uncle John : Pa Joad's older brother (Tom describes him as "a fella about 60", but in narrative he is described as 50).
- **S55**：He feels guilty about the death of his young wife years before, and is prone to binges involving alcohol and prostitutes, but is generous with his goods.
- **S56**：Jim Casy : a former preacher who lost his faith and later is murdered by a camp guard.
- **S57**：He is a Christ-like figure , based on Steinbeck's friend Ed Ricketts .
- **S58**：Al Joad : the third youngest Joad son, a "smart-aleck sixteen-year-older" who cares mainly for cars and girls; he looks up to Tom, but begins to find his own way.
- **S59**：Rose of Sharon (said as Rosasharn ) Joad Rivers : the eldest Joad daughter, a childish and dreamy teenage girl, age 18, who develops into a mature woman.
- **S60**：Married at the beginning of the novel, her husband, Connie, abandons her while she is pregnant; she eventually delivers a stillborn baby, perhaps due to malnutrition, but she uses her breast milk to save a man from starvation.
- **S61**：Connie Rivers : Rose of Sharon's husband.
- **S62**：Nineteen years old and naïve, he is overwhelmed by marriage and impending fatherhood.
- **S63**：He abandons his wife and the Joad family shortly after they arrive in California.
- **S64**：Noah Joad : the eldest Joad son, he is the first to leave the family, near Needles, California , planning to live off fishing on the Colorado River .
- **S65**：Injured at birth and described as "strange", it is implied he has slight learning difficulties.
