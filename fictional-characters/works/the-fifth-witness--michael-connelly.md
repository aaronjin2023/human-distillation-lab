- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] Mickey Haller, a Los Angeles defense attorney , takes on the case of Lisa Trammel, a long-standing client accused of murdering wealthy real estate developer Mitchell Bondurant.
- **S3**：Bondurant was struck on the crown of his head with a hammer while standing upright.
- **S4**：His height of six feet two inches raises questions about the attack and whether Trammel could have delivered the fatal blow.
- **S5**：Haller must challenge the prosecution's account and persuade the jury that the evidence does not establish her guilt.
- **S6**：Haller receives help from his former wife, Lorna Taylor, and his investigator, Dennis "Cisco" Wojciechowski, who is married to Lorna.
- **S7**：Together, they examine the evidence and search for other people who might have had reasons to kill Bondurant.
- **S8**：The prosecution is led by Andrea Freeman, an attorney Haller has never defeated in court.
- **S9**：Their legal contest forces Haller to examine each piece of evidence carefully and test the claims made against his client.
- **S10**：As the defense team examines Bondurant's affairs, Haller learns that the developer may have had ties to organized crime .
- **S11**：This possibility opens another line of inquiry, giving the defense grounds to question whether Trammel was the only person with a motive to kill him.
- **S12**：Forensic findings also cast doubt on the prosecution's account, indicating that Trammel may lack the physical ability to strike Bondurant in the manner described.
- **S13**：Haller builds his defense around these doubts and seeks to establish that another person could have committed the crime.
- **S14**：The trial turns on a witness (Louis Opparizio) whose criminal associations could point toward an alternative suspect.
- **S15**：Haller carefully questions the witness, leading him to invoke the Fifth Amendment , which protects individuals from being compelled to incriminate themselves.
- **S16**：The witness refuses to answer further questions before Haller can present the full extent of his criminal ties.
- **S17**：The judge admonishes the jury to disregard the testimony, leaving the defense with a difficult legal problem.
- **S18**：Despite this instruction, the testimony has raised doubts about who killed Bondurant.
- **S19**：The jury finds Trammel not guilty.
- **S20**：After the acquittal, Haller comes to believe that Trammel did commit the murder.
- **S21**：When he confronts her, her lack of concern unsettles him and forces him to reconsider the meaning of his victory.
- **S22**：Winning in court has freed his client from conviction, but it has not brought him the certainty or satisfaction he expected.
- **S23**：Three weeks later, Haller's practice prospers following the trial.
- **S24**：Trammel calls him again, accusing him of alerting the police to search her garden and asking him to defend her in the case concerning her husband's murder.
- **S25**：Haller refuses to represent her.
- **S26**：He tells her that he has filed to run for Los Angeles County District Attorney , marking a change in his professional direction.
- **S27**：His decision reflects his unwillingness to continue representing clients whose conduct he can no longer accept.
- **S28**：The case leaves Haller facing difficult questions about justice, legal ethics, and the difference between proving innocence in court and believing someone is innocent.
