## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] The book begins with the narrator reflecting back on the first drawing he made as a six-year-old of a boa constrictor who had eaten an elephant.
- **S3**：Upon showing the picture to grown-ups, he was prompted to create his second drawing of the inside of the boa constrictor to prove the first drawing was not a hat.
- **S4**：At this point, he was advised by the grown-up to put away his drawings and pursue something practical.
- **S5**：As an adult, he kept his first drawing with him and showed it to new acquaintances to determine if the grown-up was as enlightened as a child.
- **S6**：But the adults always reply that the picture was of a hat, so he learns to only talk of "reasonable" things to them, rather than the fanciful.
- **S7**：The narrator becomes an aircraft pilot, and one day, his plane crashes in the Sahara desert , far from civilization .
- **S8**：The narrator must fix his airplane before his supply of water runs out.
- **S9**：Here, he is greeted by a young boy who is named "the little prince." The prince asks the narrator to draw a sheep.
- **S10**：The narrator first shows him the picture of the elephant inside the snake, which, to the narrator's surprise, the prince interprets correctly.
- **S11**：After three failed attempts at drawing a sheep, the frustrated narrator draws a crate, claiming the sheep is inside.
- **S12**：Surprisingly, this drawing delights the little prince.
- **S13**：Over the course of days, while the narrator attempts to repair his plane, the prince recounts his life story.
- **S14**：He used to live on a house-sized asteroid known on Earth as "B-612".
- **S15**：The narrator recounts a story of a Turkish astronomer who, after discovering asteroid B-612, gave a presentation on his findings, but was ignored due to his traditional Turkish clothing.
- **S16**：After dressing in European garments, however, namely a suit, his discoveries were accepted.
- **S17**：The asteroid has three minuscule volcanoes (two active, and one dormant or extinct ) and various plants.
- **S18**：The prince used to clean the volcanoes and weed unwanted seeds and sprigs that infested his soil, pulling out baobab trees that were constantly on the verge of overrunning the surface due to their size.
- **S19**：The prince wants a sheep to eat the undesirable plants, but worries it will also eat plants with thorns.
- **S20**：The prince then describes a rose that grew on his asteroid.
- **S21**：The rose exaggerated ailments and coughed to have the prince care for her.
- **S22**：So, the prince made a screen and a glass globe to protect her from the cold and wind, watered her, and kept the caterpillars from eating her.
- **S23**：Despite falling in love with the rose, the prince also began to feel that she was taking advantage of him, and so he resolved to leave the planet to explore the rest of the universe.
- **S24**：Upon saying their goodbyes, the rose apologized for failing to show that she loved him.
- **S25**：She wished him well and turned down his desire to leave her in the glass globe, saying she would protect herself.
- **S26**：The prince laments that he does not understand how to love his rose while being with her.
- **S27**：After leaving his planet, the prince visited six other planets, each of which was inhabited by one adult: A king who claims that everyone is his subject, who only issues orders that can be followed within reason, such as commanding the sun to set, but only at sunset where it can reasonably do so.
- **S28**：A conceited man who only wants to be the most admired person on his otherwise uninhabited planet.
- **S29**：A tippler who drinks to forget the shame of drinking.
- **S30**：A businessman who is blind to the beauty of the stars and instead endlessly counts and catalogs them in order to "own" them all.
- **S31**：A lamplighter on a planet so small that a full day lasts only a minute.
- **S32**：He wastes his life following orders to extinguish and relight the lamp post every 30 seconds to correspond with his planet's day and night.
- **S33**：The Prince, having mentioned earlier that he loves sunsets, notes that he wishes he could have stayed on this planet the most, as the planet was blessed with 1440 sunsets a day.
- **S34**：An elderly geographer who knows nothing about his planet because he has never been anywhere or seen any of the things he records, believing instead that his role is to write down what the explorer sees, of which none exist on his planet.
- **S35**：He persuades the prince to visit Earth next.
- **S36**：Landing in a desert, the prince believed that Earth was uninhabited.
- **S37**：He then met a snake that claimed to have the power to return him to his home, if he ever wished that.
- **S38**：The prince next met a flower, who said that she had only seen a few men in that part of the world, and that they had no roots, letting the wind blow them around and living hard lives.
- **S39**：After climbing the highest mountain he had ever seen, the prince hoped to see the whole of Earth, thus finding the people; however, he saw only the desolate landscape.
- **S40**：When the prince called out, his echo answered him, which he interpreted as the voice of someone boring who only repeats words.
- **S41**：The prince encountered a row of rosebushes, becoming downcast at having once thought that his rose was unique and thinking she had lied about being unique.
- **S42**：He began to feel that he was not a great prince, as his planet contained only three tiny volcanoes and a flower he now thought of as common.
- **S43**：He started weeping until a fox came along.
- **S44**：The fox desired to be tamed and taught the prince how to tame him.
- **S45**：By being tamed, something goes from being ordinary or commonplace to being special and unique... but "We only understand what we tame." From the fox, the prince learns that his rose was indeed special because she was the object of the prince's love and time; he had "tamed" her, and now she was more precious than all of the other roses.
- **S46**：Upon their departure, the fox says that important things can only be seen with the heart, not the eyes.
- **S47**：The prince then met two people from Earth: A railway switchman who described how passengers constantly rushed from one place to another aboard trains, never satisfied with where they were and not knowing what they were after; only the children among them ever bothered to look out the windows.
- **S48**：A merchant who spoke about his product, a pill that eliminated the need to drink for a week, saving people 53 minutes.
- **S49**：Eight days after the plane crash, the narrator and the prince are dying of thirst.
- **S50**：The prince becomes morose and longs to return home and see his flower.
- **S51**：Although losing hope, the prince insists that “Somewhere in the desert there is a well,” urging the narrator to continue.
- **S52**：And at dawn, the narrator finds the well, saving them.
- **S53**：The prince reminds the narrator that his plane must still be fixed, leaving him there until the work is done.
- **S54**：Upon successfully repairing the plane and returning to the prince, the narrator finds the prince talking to the snake, discussing his return home and his desire to see his rose again, worrying that she has been left to fend for herself.
- **S55**：The prince bids farewell to the narrator and states that if it looks as though he has died, it is only because his body is too heavy to take with him to his planet.
- **S56**：The prince warns the narrator not to watch him leave, as it will upset him.
- **S57**：The narrator, realizing what will happen, refuses to leave the prince's side.
- **S58**：The prince tells the narrator that he only needs to look at the stars to think of the prince's laughter and that it will seem as if all the stars are laughing.
- **S59**：The prince then walks away and allows the snake to bite him, falling down.
- **S60**：The next morning, the narrator cannot find the prince's body.
- **S61**：Managing to repair his airplane, he leaves the desert.
- **S62**：The narrator ends his story by addressing the reader directly, requesting to be contacted if one ever encounters a boy like the prince.
