- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] Okonkwo is a famous man in Umuofia.
- **S3**：He is a wrestling champion, acclaimed warrior, and founder of a prosperous family.
- **S4**：He strives to be the opposite of his father, Unoka, an indolent debtor unable to support his wife or children, preferring flute-playing and a chronic drinking habit.
- **S5**：Okonkwo works hard from a young age to build fame and wealth all on his own.
- **S6**：Obsessed with manly strength and discipline, he often beats his wives and children.
- **S7**：Okonkwo is selected by the elders to be the guardian of Ikemefuna, a boy who was taken as a peace settlement between Umuofia and another clan after Ikemefuna's father killed a woman from Umuofia.
- **S8**：The boy looks up to Okonkwo as his second father.
- **S9**：After three years, the oracle of Umuofia pronounces that the boy must be killed.
- **S10**：Ezeudu, the village elder, warns Okonkwo to stay away from the killing, but he ignores the warning and carries out the grim task, not wanting to appear weak.
- **S11**：After killing Ikemefuna, Okonkwo feels haunted by sadness and nightmares.
- **S12**：During a gun salute at Ezeudu's funeral, Okonkwo's gun accidentally explodes and kills Ezeudu's son.
- **S13**：He and his family are exiled for seven years to his motherland, Mbanta, as required to appease the gods.
- **S14**：While Okonkwo is in Mbanta, he learns that the White men are living in Umuofia with the intent of introducing their religion, Christianity .
- **S15**：As the number of converts increases, the foothold of the White people grows and a new government is introduced.
- **S16**：Okonkwo's son Nwoye becomes curious about the missionaries, and after he is beaten by his father for the last time, he decides to leave his family to live independently.
- **S17**：Nwoye is introduced to the new religion by Mr.
- **S18**：Kiaga, an Igbo missionary.
- **S19**：In the last year of his exile, Okonkwo instructs his best friend Obierika to sell all of his yams and hire two men to build him two huts so he can have a house to go back to with his family.
- **S20**：He also holds a great feast for his mother's kinsmen.
- **S21**：Returning from Mbanta, Okonkwo finds his village changed by the presence of the White men.
- **S22**：After a Christian convert commits the crime of unmasking an elder as he embodies an ancestral spirit of the clan, the village retaliates by destroying a local Christian church.
- **S23**：In response, the District Commissioner representing the colonial government takes Okonkwo and several other native leaders prisoner pending payment of a fine of two hundred bags of cowries .
- **S24**：Despite the District Commissioner's instructions to treat the leaders of Umuofia with respect, the native "court messengers" refuse them any food or drink and humiliate them, shaving their heads, hitting them with a stick, denying bathroom facilities, and whipping them while also extracting an additional fifty bags of cowries from the payment.
- **S25**：Outraged, the people of Umuofia finally gather to decide on a course of action regarding the foreigners.
- **S26**：Okonkwo, being a warrior by nature and adamant about following the custom and tradition of Umuofia, despises all cowardice and advocates war.
- **S27**：When messengers of the White government try to stop the meeting, Okonkwo beheads the head messenger who ordered the people to disperse.
- **S28**：Because the crowd allows the other messengers to escape and does not fight alongside Okonkwo, he realises in despair that the people of Umuofia will not fight to protect themselves or their religion.
- **S29**：Later, when the District Commissioner comes to Okonkwo's house to take him to court, he finds that Okonkwo killed himself.
- **S30**：Among his own people, Okonkwo's actions have tarnished his reputation and status, as it is strictly against the teachings of the Igbo to commit suicide.
- **S31**：As the District Commissioner and his men prepare to bury Okonkwo, he muses that Okonkwo's death will make an interesting chapter for his book, The Pacification of the Primitive Tribes of the Lower Niger .
