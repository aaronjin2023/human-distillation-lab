- **性格倾向**：在困顿生活中，她倾向于沉默务实，以持续劳作应对艰难处境。（行为推断；S5、S47、S50）
- **核心动机**：杀死新生女儿时，她意在免其受苦，并提高其余家人的生存机会。（摘要直接叙述；S9）
- **价值取舍**：面对偷来的食物，她倾向于优先满足生存需要，而非拒绝食用。（行为推断；S20、S21、S22）
- **沟通方式**：人物条目明确描述她寡言，但不足以判断具体语气与表达习惯。（摘要直接叙述；S47）

## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] The story begins on Wang Lung 's wedding day and follows the rise and fall of his fortunes.
- **S3**：The House of Hwang, a family of wealthy landowners, lives in the nearby town where Wang Lung's future wife O-Lan lives as a slave.
- **S4**：However, the House of Hwang slowly declines due to opium use, frequent spending, uncontrolled borrowing, and a general unwillingness to work.
- **S5**：Following the marriage of Wang Lung and O-Lan, both work hard on their farm and slowly save enough money to buy one plot of land at a time from the Hwang family.
- **S6**：O-Lan delivers three sons and three daughters.
- **S7**：Their first daughter becomes mentally handicapped as a result of severe malnutrition brought on by famine.
- **S8**：Her father greatly pities her and calls her "Poor Fool", a name by which she is addressed throughout her life.
- **S9**：O-Lan kills her second daughter at birth to spare her the misery of growing up in such hard times and to give the remaining family a better chance to survive.
- **S10**：During the devastating famine and drought, the family must flee to a large city in Kiangsu ( Jiangsu ) to find work.
- **S11**：Wang Lung's malevolent uncle offers to buy his possessions and property but for significantly less than their value.
- **S12**：The family sells everything except the land and the house.
- **S13**：After Wang Lung discovers that the "firewagon" (the Chinese word for the newly built train) transports people south for a fee, he then faces a long journey south instead, contemplating how the family will survive walking the entire distance.
- **S14**：In the city O-Lan and the children beg, while Wang Lung pulls a rickshaw .
- **S15**：Wang Lung's father also begs, but because he is not successful at all, he just sits looking at the city life.
- **S16**：They find themselves aliens among their more metropolitan countrymen who look different and speak in a fast accent.
- **S17**：They no longer starve, thanks to the one-cent charitable meals of congee , but still live in abject poverty.
- **S18**：Wang longs to return to his land.
- **S19**：When armies approach the city, he can only work at night hauling merchandise out of fear of being conscripted.
- **S20**：At one point, his son brings home stolen meat.
- **S21**：Furious, Wang Lung throws the meat on the ground, not wanting his sons to grow up to be thieves.
- **S22**：O-Lan, however, calmly picks up the meat and cooks it.
- **S23**：When a food riot erupts, Wang Lung is swept up in a mob that is looting a rich man's house and corners the man himself.
- **S24**：Fearing for his life, the man offers Wang Lung all his money in exchange for his safety.
- **S25**：O-Lan finds a cache of jewels elsewhere in the house and takes them for herself.
- **S26**：Wang Lung uses this money to bring the family home, buy a new ox and farm tools, and hire servants to work the land for him.
- **S27**：In time, two more children are born, a twin son and daughter.
- **S28**：When he discovers the jewels that O-Lan looted, Wang Lung uses them to purchase the House of Hwang's remaining land.
- **S29**：He later sends his two elder sons to school, apprentices the second son to a merchant, and retains the third on the land.
- **S30**：As Wang Lung becomes more prosperous, he buys a concubine named Lotus.
- **S31**：O-Lan endures the betrayal of her husband when he takes the only jewels she had asked to keep for herself, two pearls, so that he can make them into earrings to present to Lotus.
- **S32**：O-Lan's health and morale deteriorate, and she eventually dies just after witnessing her first son's wedding.
- **S33**：Wang Lung finally appreciates her place in his life as he mourns her death.
- **S34**：Wang Lung and his family move into town and rent the old House of Hwang.
- **S35**：Now an old man, he desires peace within his family but is annoyed by constant disputes, especially between his first and second sons and their wives.
- **S36**：Wang Lung's third son runs away to become a soldier.
- **S37**：At the end of the novel, Wang Lung overhears his sons planning to sell the land and tries to dissuade them.
- **S38**：They say they will do as he wishes but smile knowingly at each other.
- **S39**：## Characters
- **S40**：[ edit ] Wang Lung – This poor, hard-working farmer, born and raised in a small village of Anhwei (written as Anhui in pinyin), is the protagonist of the story and suffers hardships as he accumulates wealth and the outward signs of success.
- **S41**：He has a strong sense of morality and adheres to Chinese traditions such as filial piety and duty to family.
- **S42**：He believes that the land is the source of his happiness and wealth.
- **S43**：By the end of his life he has become a very successful man and possesses a large plot of land which he buys from the House of Hwang.
- **S44**：As his lifestyle changes, he begins to indulge in the pleasures his wealth can buy—he purchases a concubine named Lotus.
- **S45**：In Pinyin , Wang's name is written Wang Long. [ 4 ] Wang is likely to be the common surname represented by the character 王 (meaning king ).
- **S46**：O-Lan – Wang Lung's first wife, formerly a slave in the House of Hwang.
- **S47**：A woman of few words, she is uneducated and yet valuable to Wang Lung for her skills, good sense, and indomitable work ethic.
- **S48**：She is considered plain or ugly; her feet are not bound.
- **S49**：Wang Lung sometimes mentions her wide lips.
- **S50**：Nevertheless, she is hardworking and self-sacrificing.
- **S51**：Toward the end of the book, O-Lan dies due to failing organs.
- **S52**：When she lies on her deathbed, Wang Lung pays all of his attention to her and purchases her coffin not long before her death.
- **S53**：Wang Lung's father – An old, parsimonious senior who seems to only want his tea, food, and grandsons.
- **S54**：He desires grandchildren to comfort him in his old age and becomes exceedingly needy and senile as the novel progresses.
- **S55**：He has strong and outdated morals.
- **S56**：Wang Lung and O-Lan's children [ edit ] Nung En (Eldest Son) – A tall and goodly boy of whom Wang Lung is very proud.
- **S57**：He grows up as a scholar and goes through a rebellious phase before Wang Lung sends him south for three years to complete his education.
- **S58**：He matures into a large and handsome man, and he marries the daughter of the local grain merchant Liu.
- **S59**：As his father's position continues to rise, Nung En becomes increasingly enamored with wealth, and he wishes to have a showy and rich life.
- **S60**：He is also the antagonist of the film.
- **S61**：Nung Wen (Middle Son) – Wang Lung's clever son.
- **S62**：He has a shrewd mind for business but goes against his father's traditional ethics.
- **S63**：He is described as crafty, thin, and wise with money, and he's far more thrifty than Nung En.
- **S64**：Nung Wen becomes a merchant and weds a village girl, since he thinks women from the town are too vain.
- **S65**：The Poor Fool – O-Lan and Wang Lung's eldest daughter and third child whose mental handicap may have been caused by severe starvation during her infancy.
- **S66**：As the years go by, Wang Lung grows very fond of her.
- **S67**：She mostly sits in the sun and twists a piece of cloth.
- **S68**：By the time of Wang Lung's death, his concubine Pear Blossom (see below) has taken charge of caring for her.
- **S69**：Second Baby Girl – Killed immediately after delivery.
- **S70**：Third Daughter – The twin of the youngest son.
- **S71**：She is described as a pretty child with an almond flower-colored face and thin red lips.
- **S72**：During the story, her feet are bound.
- **S73**：She is betrothed to the son of a merchant (her sister-in-law's family) at age 9 and moves to their home at age 13 to escape the harassment by Wang Lung's cousin.
- **S74**：Youngest Son – Put in charge of the fields while the middle and eldest sons go to school.
- **S75**：He grows up to be an independent person and runs away against his father's wishes to become a soldier.
- **S76**：Eldest Son's Wife – Daughter of a grain merchant and a townswoman who hates the middle son's wife because she seems lower class.
- **S77**：She is brought to the house before O-Lan's death and is deemed proper and fit by the dying woman.
- **S78**：Her first child is a boy.
- **S79**：Middle Son's Wife – A woman from the village who hates the first son's wife for her snobbery and rudeness.
- **S80**：Her first child is a girl.
- **S81**：Wang Lung's concubines and servants [ edit ] Lotus Flower – Loved by Wang Lung, she is the much-spoiled concubine and former prostitute who eventually becomes old, fat, and less attractive from using tobacco and eating fattening foods.
- **S82**：She helps arrange the eldest son's and youngest daughter's marriages.
- **S83**：Cuckoo – Formerly a slave in the Hwang household, she becomes the madame of the "tea house", then eventually a servant to Lotus.
- **S84**：Hated by O-Lan because she was cruel to her in the Hwang house.
- **S85**：Pear Blossom – Bought as a young girl, she serves as a slave to Lotus.
- **S86**：At the end of the novel, she becomes Wang Lung's concubine because she says she prefers the quiet devotion of old men to the fiery passions of younger men.
- **S87**：Ching – Wang Lung's faithful friend and neighbour.
- **S88**：He shares a few beans with Wang Lung during the famine to save O-Lan's life.
- **S89**：After Ching's wife dies of famine, he is forced to give his daughter away and sells his land to Wang Lung, ultimately working for Wang as his foreman.
- **S90**：Ching dies from an accident in the fields when he shows a fellow farmer how to thresh grain.
- **S91**：Wang Lung buries him just outside the entrance to the family graveyard and orders that his own grave be placed within the perimeter but as close to Ching as possible.
- **S92**：Extended family line [ edit ] Wang Lung's Uncle – A sly, lazy man who is secretly one of the leaders of a band of thieves known as the Redbeards.
- **S93**：He caused trouble for Wang Lung and others in the household for many years, until eventually Wang Lung gives him enough opium to keep him in a harmless stupor for the rest of his life.
- **S94**：He is described as skinny, gaunt, and very self-defensive.
- **S95**：He takes advantage of the tradition that requires younger generations to care for their elders but completely disregards any moral obligations on his part.
- **S96**：Uncle's Wife – She becomes a friend to Lotus; also becomes addicted to opium.
- **S97**：Very fat, greedy, and lazy.
- **S98**：Uncle's Son – Wild and lazy, he leads Nung En into trouble and eventually becomes a soldier.
- **S99**：He is disrespectful, visits many concubines, and can be described as a sexual predator.
