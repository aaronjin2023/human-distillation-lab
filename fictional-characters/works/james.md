## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Characters
- **S2**：[ edit ] Jim (James) – The novel's eponymous protagonist and first-person narrator, Jim is an approximately 27-year-old enslaved man owned by Miss Watson.
- **S3**：The narrative's inciting incident occurs when Jim learns of Miss Watson's plan to sell him, and he determines to emancipate himself and, eventually, his family.
- **S4**：Unlike in Adventures of Huckleberry Finn , where he is depicted as simple, credulous, and superstitious, Everett’s Jim is skeptical, deeply calculating, and secretly more literate and erudite than most of the white people around him.
- **S5**：Indeed, Everett's Jim is an ironic inversion of Twain's, insofar as the near totality of Jim's personality in Twain's novel is revealed, in Everett's novel, to be a self-preservationist act put on to avert white suspicion.
- **S6**：Jim carefully performs the role expected of him by white society while finding his own covert ways to resist.
- **S7**：It is only near the end of the novel that Jim directly reveals himself to be Huck's biological father.
- **S8**：Huckleberry Finn (Huck) – A young, rebellious white-passing boy who escapes from the abusive Pap.
- **S9**：While part of a racist society, Huck is more open-minded than other white characters.
- **S10**：Both Twain's and Everett's novels suggest that Huck's relative (though not complete) lack of bigotry is at least partly due to how he has lived much of his life as something of an outsider to society as a whole, and so he has not been as thoroughly indoctrinated by Southern white ideals.
- **S11**：He develops a strong bond with Jim, which grows deeper and more complicated when he learns that Jim is his biological father.
- **S12**：Huck's journey is as much about moral awakening as it is about survival, and the discovery of his Black heritage forces him to reckon with the arbitrary and hollow nature of white racial prejudice.
- **S13**：The King and the Duke – A pair of white con men who invite themselves along for part of the journey.
- **S14**：Their real names are never divulged but, in an effort to enthrall Jim and Huck, they pose as the lost Dauphin of France and the lost Duke of Bridgewater .
- **S15**：The two charlatans are largely defined by their callous opportunism, and they ultimately betray Jim by trying to sell him.
- **S16**：These two characters also feature in Twain's novel, but Everett draws a more detailed and sinister portrait through Jim's perspective.
- **S17**：Daniel Decatur Emmett – A real historical figure and founder of one of the first minstrel troupes in the U.S.
- **S18**：In the novel, he purchases Jim to perform in his troupe, but Jim escapes and purloins Emmett's notebook to continue recording his own story, symbolizing Jim's journey towards self-expression and reclaimed identity.
- **S19**：Before losing the notebook, Emmett used it to compose songs for the minstrel shows; some of his material appears in the opening of the novel, which begins with "The Notebook of Daniel Decatur Emmett", featuring lyrics from various songs.
- **S20**：Norman – A former slave who, as a free man, passes for white.
- **S21**：He illustrates a conflicted experience of " passing ", which carries certain advantages for Norman but that he describes as "exhausting". [ 3 ] Norman is part of the traveling minstrel show run by Emmett, who briefly purchases (or, as Emmett tries to frame it, "hires") Jim.
- **S22**：After Norman helps Jim escape from his exploitative captor, he becomes one of Jim's closest allies, but he ultimately dies in a riverboat accident.
- **S23**：Sammy – An enslaved teenage girl who briefly escapes the lumber mill with Jim and Norman.
- **S24**：When Jim first meets the taciturn Sammy, he mistakes her for a young man; upon learning otherwise, he identifies her with his own daughter and feels compelled to take her along when he escapes the mill with Norman.
- **S25**：Tragically, Sammy is murdered by their white pursuers.
- **S26**：Judge Thatcher – The local judge who, in Adventures of Huckleberry Finn , helps protect Huck's money by keeping it away from the greedy and abusive Pap.
- **S27**：While Twain's novel depicts Judge Thatcher as a kind and fair man who contrasts with the corruption and cruelty found in other parts of society, Everett's reinvention of the character presents more ambiguity as it explores the judge's complicity in the inhumane system of slavery.
- **S28**：One of the judge's most salient narrative roles is indirect, in the form of his extensive library that has allowed Jim to secretly educate himself over the years.
- **S29**：Judge Thatcher is among the novel's most educated characters, and he is shocked and intimidated to eventually discover Jim's extensive learning.
- **S30**：Sadie and Lizzie – Jim’s wife and daughter.
- **S31**：Though they seldom appear directly in the narrative, they are ever-present as the driving force behind Jim's actions as he endeavors to reunite with his family.
- **S32**：These names for Jim's family originated in the 2005 novel My Jim by Nancy Rawles .
- **S33**：Twain tells the story of Jim's escape through the eyes of Huckleberry Finn.
- **S34**：My Jim tells it from the perspective of Sadie, and James from the perspective of a reimagined Jim.
