以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] The clandestine mission of the Dutch ship Erasmus was to compete with Portugal for Japan and the rest of the lucrative far East Asian trade.
- **S3**：After much of its crew, including its captain, dies, it ends up marooned in Izu Harbor, the survivors thereby becoming the first Protestants to set foot in Japan.
- **S4**：The ship's crew is held captive while armaments, records and coin are seized by Izu's daimyō (lord) Yabu.
- **S5**：Yabu had hoped to keep the ship a secret, but a spy reported the ship's arrival to his liege Toranaga, Lord of the Kantō and President of the Council of Regents.
- **S6**：Toranaga has the ship's navigator John Blackthorne brought to him in Osaka, knowing that the Erasmus could be a source of advantages against Lord Ishido Kazunari, his chief rival in the council.
- **S7**：Toranaga's meeting with Blackthorne is faithfully translated by a Portuguese Jesuit, Father Martin Alvito, despite revelations of war between Catholic Portugal and Elizabethan England.
- **S8**：Until then Toranaga was unaware that Christendom was so divided.
- **S9**：To sequester Blackthorne from the other regents, he is imprisoned with a Franciscan friar who teaches Blackthorne rudimentary Japanese and relates how the Jesuits ' rōnin (mercenaries) invaded Japan and launched violent agitations to profit the Portuguese crown.
- **S10**：Before the two Catholic regents, urged on by the Jesuits, can have him executed, Blackthorne is abducted in transit and returned to Toranaga.
- **S11**：Lady Toda Mariko (a Jesuit-educated Catholic loyal to Toranaga, not her church) faithfully translates to Toranaga Blackthorne's account of the Pope granting Portugal colonial rights to Japan and East Asia in return for replacement of all non-Catholic lords, including Toranaga, with those loyal to Portugal and Rome.
- **S12**：He also relates the Franciscan's report of Catholic rōnin from Macau invading Japan.
- **S13**：Toranaga is taken aback and refuses Portugal's trading ship request to leave Japan.
- **S14**：In turn the regents, after once again failing to assassinate Blackthorne, try to force Toranaga to commit seppuku .
- **S15**：Instead he resigns from the council and flees Osaka.
- **S16**：Aided by Blackthorne's clownish antics, he, Toranaga, Mariko and others of his court make it to Anjiro, which is safer.
- **S17**：Toranaga elevates Blackthorne to the samurai rank of hatamoto and gifts him a consort, Fujiko.
- **S18**：In Anjiro, Blackthorne threatens to commit seppuku after Yabu says he will burn down the village if the Englishman doesn't learn Japanese fast enough.
- **S19**：He is narrowly stopped by Omi.
- **S20**：Slowly, Blackthorne's grasp of Japanese speech and customs improves.
- **S21**：He trains a contingent of samurai in European-style warfare, and after a devastating earthquake, rescues Toranaga from underneath rubble.
- **S22**：In turn Blackthorne raises his regard for Toranaga and for Mariko, a key member of Toranaga's inner circle, and with whom he secretly has an affair.
- **S23**：A chance encounter with Blackthorne's old crew finds them revolted by his Japanese ways, and he by the coarseness of their European character.
- **S24**：To deflect suspicions, Toranaga feigns to all except Mariko acquiescence towards Ishido and professes no desire to do battle.
- **S25**：When Toranaga's half-brother Zataki, who has allied with Ishido, arrives, Toranaga apparently surrenders and has Mariko re-enter Osaka with the intention to lay bare Ishido's holding of noble households hostage.
- **S26**：When, as planned, Mariko tries to exit Osaka, Ishido's men violently block her party until an intentionally unharmed Mariko gives up on leaving.
- **S27**：Saying she has been dishonored, Mariko vows to kill herself the next day.
- **S28**：She almost ends her life, but in a delaying gambit, Ishido grants her leave at the last minute.
- **S29**：That night Toranaga's duplicitous vassal Yabu lets Ishido's ninjas into Toranaga's compound to kidnap Mariko.
- **S30**：Having retreated to a storeroom, Mariko willfully stands in front of a door set to explode and is killed.
- **S31**：Her death, which Ishido sought to prevent, forces him to free his noble hostages, thus weakening military alliances.
- **S32**：As Blackthorne and Yabu leave, the Jesuits inform the former that the Erasmus has been sunk.
- **S33**：As for Yabu, he is caught out by Toranaga and obeys his lord's order to commit seppuku, giving his prized katana to Blackthorne.
- **S34**：Mariko wills money to Blackthorne to build a seaworthy ship for Toranaga's navy.
- **S35**：At the book's end, Toranaga in soliloquy says he sank the Erasmus to form alliances with the Catholic lords, who in return agreed not to kill Blackthorne.
- **S36**：Blackthorne's karma , Toranaga says, is to never leave Japan, as Mariko's karma was to die for her lord, and as Toranaga's is to become shogun.
- **S37**：The book's epilogue takes place after the Battle of Sekigahara with Toranaga burying Ishido up to his neck until he dies three days later.
