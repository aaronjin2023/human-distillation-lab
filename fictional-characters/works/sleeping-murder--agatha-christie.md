## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] Newlywed Gwenda Reed travels from New Zealand ahead of her husband to find a home in England.
- **S3**：She finds and buys Hillside, a large old house that she feels a strange connection to.
- **S4**：She supervises the renovation, discovering she has very definite ideas of what should be done.
- **S5**：When workmen open a long-sealed door, they find the very wallpaper that had been in her mind; and her request for a doorway between two rooms proves to have been such a doorway years earlier.
- **S6**：She goes to London to visit relatives: the author Raymond West, his wife, and his aunt, Miss Jane Marple .
- **S7**：During a performance of The Duchess of Malfi , at the line "Cover her face; mine eyes dazzle; she died young", Gwenda screams.
- **S8**：She sees a man saying those very words strangling a blonde-haired woman whom she thinks of as Helen.
- **S9**：Gwenda had been born in India.
- **S10**：After her mother died, she was raised in New Zealand by her mother's sister, and her father died a few years later.
- **S11**：She has memories of being on a ship as a young child.
- **S12**：Miss Marple suggests that Gwenda had lived in England with her father and his second wife, which proves to be the case.
- **S13**：Her stepmother, Helen Halliday (née Kennedy), had met her father travelling from India back to England, and their shipboard romance led to marriage upon arrival in England.
- **S14**：The coincidences prove to be memories from Gwenda's stay in Hillside 18 years ago as a young child.
- **S15**：Her husband Giles arrives from New Zealand, and the couple decide to pursue the mystery.
- **S16**：Helen had been raised mainly by her half-brother, Dr Kennedy, now retired from practice.
- **S17**：He replies to an advertisement seeking information about Helen.
- **S18**：Miss Marple finds the man who once gardened for the Kennedy family, who supplies useful descriptions of events.
- **S19**：Miss Marple also finds the cook from the Halliday household, Edith, who remembers that time well.
- **S20**：The Hallidays were soon to move to a house in Norfolk before Helen disappeared.
- **S21**：It seems that Helen had wanted to get away from someone, and the servants presumed it was her husband.
- **S22**：The Reeds advertise, seeking the Hallidays' former maid Lily.
- **S23**：She writes first to Dr Kennedy, seeking his advice.
- **S24**：She says that she does not believe that Helen ran off, as the clothes packed in her suitcase made no sense – she took an evening gown but not the shoes and belt that went with it.
- **S25**：The Reeds and Dr Kennedy agree he should write back to her to arrange a meeting.
- **S26**：Lily never arrives.
- **S27**：The police find Lily's body, strangled, in a copse near the railway station.
- **S28**：She had come by an earlier train than expected, even though she had with her Dr Kennedy's letter indicating the correct, later arrival time.
- **S29**：Soon they are digging up the garden at the end of the terrace, where they find Helen's body.
- **S30**：While Gwenda is in the house alone, Dr Kennedy approaches and attempts to strangle her.
- **S31**：Miss Marple arrives with a container of soapy solution, which she sprays in his eyes.
- **S32**：Miss Marple explains that Dr Kennedy was the person Helen had wanted to get away from; she had dearly loved Halliday and his daughter.
- **S33**：Kennedy had strangled his sister, saying the closing words from the play, unaware of young Gwenda watching from the stairs above.
- **S34**：Kennedy had previously given Halliday drugs to make him paranoid, then drugged his drink, posing him next to the strangled Helen to convince him that he was the killer.
- **S35**：Kennedy then buried Helen in the garden.
- **S36**：Since no body was ever found, Halliday's belief that he had killed Helen led him to be diagnosed as insane, and he died in a nursing home.
- **S37**：The letter found with Lily was not the one she received from Kennedy; he had switched it after he killed her.
- **S38**：After a full confession from Kennedy, Miss Marple comments that they should have seen it from the start: those words from the play had been spoken by a brother who had just killed his sister.
