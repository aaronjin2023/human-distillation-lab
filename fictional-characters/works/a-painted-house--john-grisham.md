## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot
- **S2**：[ edit ] The story begins as Luke Chandler and his grandfather Eli, also known as Pappy, search for migrant workers to help them with the cotton picking.
- **S3**：They initially consider themselves lucky to hire the Spruills, a family of " hill people ," and a few Mexican migrants who annually come to the area looking for work.
- **S4**：Aside from working long hours under the hot sun in the fields, Luke's life is fairly idyllic.
- **S5**：He is obsessed with beautiful 17-year-old Tally Spruill, who on one occasion lets him see her naked, bathing in a creek.
- **S6**：But a much more unpleasant experience is seeing Tally's brother, the overly aggressive and mentally unstable Hank Spruill, attack three boys from the notorious Sisco family, one of whom is beaten so severely that he dies from his wounds.
- **S7**：Hank arrogantly identifies Luke as a friendly witness who can support his version of the event.
- **S8**：The fearful boy backs up his story, although the adults in his life, including local sheriff Stick Powers, suspect he's too frightened to admit the truth.
- **S9**：When Luke sees Cowboy, one of the Mexicans, later murder Hank and tossing his body into the river, Cowboy threatens to kill Luke's mother if he tells anyone what he saw.
- **S10**：Cowboy and Tally then run off together and are not seen again.
- **S11**：Luke also learns that his admired Uncle Ricky, fighting in the Korean War , might have fathered a child with a daughter of the Latchers, their poverty-stricken sharecropping neighbors.
- **S12**：Grisham surrounds these dramatic moments with descriptive passages of life in the rural South and the ordinary events that fill Luke's weekly routine.
- **S13**：His hard work in the fields is preceded by a hearty breakfast of eggs, ham, biscuits, and the one cup of coffee his mother allows him, and at day's end he's rewarded with an evening on the front porch, where the family gathers around the radio to listen to Harry Caray announce the St.
- **S14**：Louis Cardinals baseball games.
- **S15**：A devoted fan, Luke is saving his hard-earned money to buy a team warm-up jacket he saw advertised in the Sears, Roebuck catalog.
- **S16**：Saturday afternoons are spent in town, where the adults share idle gossip and serious concerns and the youngsters visit the movie house, while Sunday morning is reserved for church.
- **S17**：A visiting carnival , the annual town picnic, and Luke's introduction to television – to see a live broadcast of a World Series game – are additional bits of local color scattered throughout the tale.
- **S18**：A flood devastates the family's crop before the harvest is completed, and Luke's parents decide to travel to the city to find work in a Buick plant, breaking a history of generations working on the land.
- **S19**：The novel ends with Luke's mother smiling on the bus, having finally gotten her wish to leave cotton farming.
- **S20**：The book's title refers to the Chandler house, which never has been painted, a sign of their lower social status in the community.
- **S21**：One day Luke discovers that someone has been secretly painting the weather-beaten clapboards white, and eventually he continues the job with the approval of his parents and the assistance of the Mexicans, contributing some of his own savings for the purchase of paint.
