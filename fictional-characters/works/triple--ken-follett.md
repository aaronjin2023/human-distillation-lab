## 引用证据

以下保留提炼与审校所用的完整摘要选摘，包括未被维度判断直接引用的句子，以保全综合笔记的上下文。

- **S1**：## Plot Summary
- **S2**：[ edit ] The book's prologue describes a chance meeting of several people in Oxford University in 1947.
- **S3**：The main character is Nathaniel "Nat" Dickstein, a victim of experimentation in the Nazi concentration camps who later becomes an Israeli agent; also the American Alan "Al" Cortone, of Italian origin, whose life Dickstein saved in Sicily; and the Russian chessmaster David Rostov, who subsequently joins the KGB .
- **S4**：The three go together to a sherry party given by Professor Ashford, who teaches Hebrew Literature at Oxford .
- **S5**：Dickstein, who is studying under Ashford, is in love with his Lebanese wife Eila, but there are rival suitors.
- **S6**：Another guest, Yasif Hassan, of Palestinian origin, has an affair with Eila, as later seen in Professor Ashford's garden.
- **S7**：Ashford's daughter, Suza, also makes an appearance.
- **S8**：In the first chapters Pierre Bourg, the head of the Mossad , finds out in 1968 that Egypt is building a nuclear reactor in the Western Desert , in order to produce a nuclear bomb.
- **S9**：He assigns his best agent, Nat Dickstein, to steal about two hundred tons of uranium ore for Israel, to pre-empt the potential Egyptian threat and to enable them to build a nuclear bomb themselves .
- **S10**：Dickstein has to ensure that nobody suspects Israeli participation in the theft.
- **S11**：Dickstein travels to Luxembourg to obtain documentation on all uranium shipments from the EURATOM agency located there.
- **S12**：He eventually achieves this by blackmailing a EURATOM employee.
- **S13**：In his hotel he has a chance encounter with Yasif Hassan, who is now an Egyptian Mukhabarat agent.
- **S14**：As a result of this the KGB gets onto Dickstein's tail.
- **S15**：For practical reasons, and also because in 1948 he successfully hijacked an arms shipment at sea, Dickstein decides to make the uranium theft from a maritime transport of yellowcake ore, from a freighter called the Coparelli .
- **S16**：Dickstein is persistently followed by Rostov's KGB group, to which Hassan now also belongs.
- **S17**：This culminates in the tailing of all Israeli diplomats in London by the KGB.
- **S18**：Despite this, Dickstein succeeds in repeatedly shaking off his pursuers.
- **S19**：He travels to Oxford to see Professor Ashford again.
- **S20**：Instead of his former teacher, the now grown-up daughter Suza Ashford answers the door.
- **S21**：They have a candid conversation and fall in love.
- **S22**：A short time later, Hassan visits the Professor (whose sympathies were already pro-Arab), and tells him of Dickstein's true activities and plans.
- **S23**：The two persuade Suza to help eliminate Dickstein.
- **S24**：She pretends to go along with this in order to be in a better position to warn her lover of this danger.
- **S25**：Hassan also intends to deliver the uranium to the Palestinian Fedayeen , betraying both Rostov and the Egyptians.
- **S26**：To facilitate the robbery at sea, Dickstein acquires the Stromberg , a sister ship of the Coparelli , and founds a bogus maritime company.
- **S27**：He also arranges for the Coparelli to suffer a mechanical breakdown at sea, and for the crew to be almost completely disembarked.
- **S28**：Through further complicated measures, Dickstein hopes to erase traces of the uranium theft.
- **S29**：Israeli naval commandos aboard the Stromberg are to attack the Coparelli , but Hassan and his Fedayeen arrive first.
- **S30**：However Dickstein and the Israelis recapture the Coparelli , and Hassan is killed.
- **S31**：Dickstein then goes alone to board the Soviet ship Karla , that is also in the area, as Suza Ashford is a prisoner there.
- **S32**：Rostov and a KGB force are aboard.
- **S33**：Suza is able to create a diversion, enabling Dickstein to rescue her and destroy the Karla by means of a magnetic mine .
- **S34**：The story ends with the statement that Dickstein has left Mossad to settle with Suza in Israel.
- **S35**：The book concludes with a newspaper article appearing in the Daily Telegraph in 1977, revealing that Israel is suspected of involvement in the disappearance at sea of the uranium shipment nine years earlier. [ 1 ]
